THE PROCESS.

The bread metaphor was given to us live — by the Q team, in the open, while the drops were still being posted. Dough, crumbs, bakers, bread. Then the carrying out.

This page explains how a drop was worked. It does not decode a drop, and it will not tell you what to think about one.

Five stages

  1. 01Dough
  2. 02Crumbs
  3. 03Bakers
  4. 04Bread
  5. 05Dissemination

You cannot teach someone to bake by handing them a loaf.

Only someone who has made bread themselves can teach another person to make it. So the process is what gets shown. The answer is left exactly where it belongs — at the end of the work, for the person who did the work.

The work, in order

Five stages, one thread

Read it from the top down. Each stage hands something to the next. Crumbs are the parts you bring back and hang on the thread so the next person can pick them up.

  1. 01 / 05

    Dough

    The drop itself. The raw primary material, exactly as it was posted, untouched.

    What you actually do

    You read it where it landed, in the order it landed. You keep the words, the number, the date and the board it was posted to. You do not tidy it up, you do not explain it, and you do not decide yet what it is for. If a link in it is dead today, you say the link is dead — you don't quietly swap in a new one.

  2. 02 / 05

    Crumbs

    The research sitting underneath a drop: the sourcing, the links, the images, and the questions the drop itself asked. This is the anons' work.

    What you actually do

    You follow what the drop points at and bring back what you find. You label where each piece came from. Crumbs are not conclusions — they are what you can point to.

    • the link the drop pointed at
    • the picture from that day
    • the public record it names
    • the question the drop asked
    • what the boards were saying around it
    • what did not check out

    Small things, each one traceable. On their own they prove nothing. Together they are what a loaf is later made from.

  3. 03 / 05

    Bakers

    The anon decoders who do that work. Not a title anybody hands out — the people who turned up, drop after drop, and did the reading.

    What you actually do

    You pick up a drop and carry it as far as you honestly can, and you show your working so the next person can check it, add to it, or correct it. Nobody bakes alone and nobody is the authority. The work is only worth something if it survives being checked by other people.

  4. 04 / 05

    Bread

    The cumulative result: Q intel built up across drops, answers to questions the drops asked, the work of many hands in one place. The finished thing a person can pick up.

    What you actually do

    You bring the work together and keep the joins visible — which drop, which crumb, which person. You don't hand over a loaf with the recipe torn off. Bread is not the same thing as a conclusion: it is what can be picked up and carried.

  5. 05 / 05

    Dissemination

    The final step, and not a bake at all. Anons carrying the bread out across the internet and social media.

    What you actually do

    You take what checks out in real life — data, facts, events that were never reported or told to the public — and you put it where people will see it, past the mainstream filter. You keep it traceable back to the dough. You don't dress it up, and you don't ask anybody to take your word for it.

The witness

Why I tell it this way

The Rubber Duck™ @TheRubberDuck79

I'm a storyteller, and a visual learner and teacher — because pictures and stories are the only way I have ever been able to learn anything. Not dressed up. Just the story told properly, with the nuance left in.

I'm autistic — diagnosed fifty years ago — with severe adult ADD. That is why the Q team nicknamed our crowd "autists" early on. They knew exactly who they were talking to.

I don't decode drops for people, and most drops never needed decoding. What I have is the part that cannot be duplicated: I was there while it was happening. Waiting for the next drop to land. Reading the board while it was still warm. Only a small number of people experienced that.

So I won't hand you a loaf. I'll show you the five stages instead, and you'll do your own baking.

The reveal is arrived at, not delivered.

That is the whole design of it. Nobody can do the work for you, and nobody should. What this page can do is show you where to start, and what to bring back when you've done the reading.
