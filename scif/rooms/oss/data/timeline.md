# OSS Secret Room: timeline in plain words

Every line below comes from a document in Mike's `OSS` folder (file named in brackets). R2 copies are at https://memes.therubberduck.net/scif/rooms/oss/pdfs/.

**June 13, 1942: the OSS is born.** President Roosevelt orders the creation of the Office of Strategic Services and names William J. "Wild Bill" Donovan its director. [FDR-Order-Establishing-OSS-1942-06-13]

**January 17, 1944: the sabotage manual.** The OSS issues its Simple Sabotage Field Manual, a how-to for ordinary people slowing down an enemy from the inside. [OSS-Simple-Sabotage-Field-Manual-1944]

**November 18, 1944: Donovan asks for a permanent spy service.** Donovan writes to Roosevelt asking for a peacetime central intelligence service that reports straight to the President, with no police powers and no operations inside the U.S. [CIA-Creation-of-the-Intelligence-Community-Founding-Documents]

**September 20, 1945: Truman shuts the OSS down.** Executive Order 9621 ends the OSS a few weeks after the war. Its research branch goes to the State Department and the rest goes to the War Department. It was published in the Federal Register on September 22. (EO 9620, signed the same day, is a different order. It isn't the OSS one.) [EO-9621-OSS-termination-official-text-1945-09-20; EO-9621-Federal-Register-1945-09-22-scan-pages]

**July 26, 1947: the CIA is created.** The National Security Act of 1947 creates the CIA, the National Security Council, and the National Military Establishment. [National-Security-Act-1947-61-Stat]

**1961: Bay of Pigs fallout.** After the failed Bay of Pigs invasion, Kennedy changes how covert work is run. On June 28, 1961, NSAM 55 tells the Joint Chiefs to advise him directly, NSAM 56 orders a count of paramilitary assets, and NSAM 57 gives big paramilitary operations mainly to Defense, with the CIA supporting. [NSAM-55, NSAM-56, NSAM-57]

**November 28, 1961: Dulles leaves the CIA.** Kennedy gives Allen Dulles an award as he departs. The public record is a medal ceremony, not a firing speech. [JFK-remarks-award-to-Allen-Dulles-1961-11-28]

**March 13, 1962: Operation Northwoods.** The Joint Chiefs propose staged pretexts for a war with Cuba. The plan was not approved. [Operation-Northwoods-JCS-memo-1962-03-13]

**December 22, 1963: Truman's warning.** One month after Dallas, Truman writes in The Washington Post that he never meant the CIA to run peacetime cloak-and-dagger operations. The article is copyrighted, so it's linked only. [SOURCES.md]

**April 25, 1966: the "thousand pieces" line.** The New York Times quotes an unnamed "highest official" of the Kennedy administration saying JFK wanted to "splinter the CIA into a thousand pieces and scatter it to the winds." It's a published allegation, not a recorded JFK quote. [ANGLES.md; SOURCES.md]

**December 23, 2016: Congress sets the stage.** Section 922 of the FY2017 defense bill gives the Pentagon's top special operations civilian, the ASD(SO/LIC), a role like a service secretary's over special operations training, equipping, and organization. [FY2017-NDAA-Section-922-PL-114-328-excerpt]

**November 10, 2020: Ezra moves up.** Ezra Cohen-Watnick, who had been acting head of special operations policy, is named Acting Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security. [DoD-Release-Personnel-Changes-at-the-Pentagon-2020-11-10]

**November 18, 2020: Miller elevates special operations at Fort Bragg.** Acting Defense Secretary Christopher Miller signs a memo having the special operations civilian chief report directly to him, with a budget role like the Army, Navy, and Air Force secretaries'. Ezra Cohen-Watnick speaks after Miller's introduction and says Trump is "fully realizing President Kennedy's prescient view of special operations forces." Miller says he hopes Congress will make the job an under secretary. [Miller-ASD-SOLIC-organizational-role-memo-2020-11-18; DoD-News-Special-Operations-Leader-to-Report-Directly-2020-11-18]

**December 3, 2020: putting it into motion.** The Deputy Secretary of Defense orders a plan to carry out Miller's change. [DepSecDef-ASD-SOLIC-implementation-plan-memo-2020-12-03]

**May 5, 2021: Austin partly rolls it back.** Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin puts the office back under the Under Secretary for Policy for policy matters. It keeps the direct line to the Secretary only for special operations administrative matters. The Congressional Research Service calls this a reversal of Miller's decision. [Austin-ASD-SOLIC-organizational-role-memo-2021-05-05; CRS-RS21048]

**December 23, 2024: Congress pushes again.** The FY2025 defense bill tells the Pentagon to fully lock in the office's authority within a year. [FY2025-NDAA-Sections-907-and-908]

**November 2, 2026, made public November 12: Hegseth brings back the OSS name.** Secretary of War Pete Hegseth puts the office on equal footing with the military department secretaries and gives it the title "Assistant Secretary of War for the Office of Strategic Services." It gets at least $45 million a year, 100 staff slots, and its own budget and buying authority, and he asks Congress for a permanent Under Secretary of Strategic Services. It does not recreate the wartime OSS or replace the CIA, and it isn't a new military branch. [Hegseth-ASW-OSS-memorandum-2026-11-02; DoW-Release-Hegseth-Elevates-SOF-Leadership-OSS-2026-11-12]

**The thread:** 1942 OSS → 1945 shut down → 1947 CIA → 1961 Kennedy moves paramilitary work toward the military → 2016 Congress → 2020 Miller elevation → 2021 Austin rollback → 2026 Hegseth finishes it under the OSS name.
