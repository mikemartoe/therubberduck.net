President Abraham Lincoln used the military to save the republic during the American Civil War. In 1861, several Southern states seceded from the Union and formed the Confederate States of America. Lincoln believed that the United States was one nation that could not be divided, and he was determined to preserve the Union.

Lincoln used the military to suppress the rebellion and restore the authority of the federal government. He called for volunteers to join the Union Army, and he expanded the size of the army and navy. Lincoln also suspended the writ of habeas corpus in certain areas and authorized arbitrary arrests to maintain order.

In 1863, Lincoln issued the Emancipation Proclamation, which declared that all slaves in Confederate territory were to be set free. This transformed the war from a conflict to preserve the Union into a struggle for human freedom and equality.

Lincoln's military strategy was successful, and the Union Army defeated the Confederate forces in 1865. The war was a turning point in American history, and it ensured that the United States would remain one nation, indivisible.
